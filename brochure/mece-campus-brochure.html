01

Every placement season runs on the same quiet unfairness.

Two equally hardworking students walk into the same shortlist — one had seniors who ran drills with them all term, the other had a WhatsApp group and hope.

MECE began the way most placement prep does — 2 a.m. peer mocks, seniors' PDFs passed down like heirlooms, and feedback that changed depending on who was across the table. So we replaced subjectivity with a rubric: practise any hour, get the same rigorous 100-point feedback every time — the same way for the topper and the first-generation MBA alike.

02

One rubric. A hundred points. Six dimensions a panel actually grades on.

The same criteria hit every submission, so a student tracks genuine improvement instead of a mood. Written strengths and fixes come back in about a minute.

Structure25

MECE decomposition into a bespoke, logical framework; scope clarified before solving.

Quantitative20

Accurate maths, Pareto-driven prioritisation, sanity-checked against plausibility.

Synthesis20

Top-down recommendation, logic in descending importance, executive tone.

Business Judgment15

Stress-tested across macro environment, industry dynamics and company constraints.

Creativity10

Multiple distinct, testable hypotheses — structured exploration, not brainstorming.

Professional Tone10

Confident without arrogance, honest about uncertainty, ethical, humble.

Parse→ Score the rubric→ Verify the math→ Write feedback→ Rank vs the national pool

Not multiple choice. A back-and-forth interview: the student structures, the AI interviewer probes the weak link — exactly like a McKinsey or BCG round. Cases and guesstimates share one engine.

Case · Market entry

Should a South-Asian SaaS payroll firm enter Southeast Asia?

Recommend go / no-go with a clear structure, a sized opportunity, and the two risks that matter.

Framework / ContextMarket attractiveness · right-to-win · entry mode · unit economics.
Interviewer
Take a minute. How will you decide whether this market is worth entering?
Three buckets — is it attractive, can we win, can we make money. I'd size SME payroll spend first.
Good. Give me a rough size — walk me through the maths.
~6M SMEs × ~40% digitised × ₹18k/yr ≈ ₹43,000 Cr. I'll pressure-test the digitisation rate next.
